# Refine Olyxee Logistics Landing Page Section Design

Keep the existing Olyxee Logistics visual identity, but improve the way individual sections are composed.

The page should feel intentionally art-directed like a mature freight software company, not like an AI-generated SaaS landing page.

Avoid repeating the same pattern of:

heading → paragraph → 3 equal cards → button

for every section.

Each major section should have its own visual composition while still belonging to the same design system.

## Design Principle

Use **editorial rhythm**.

Alternate between:

* large statement sections
* split-screen product sections
* full-width product UI sections
* workflow/timeline sections
* dark emphasis sections
* customer proof sections
* asymmetric layouts
* compact information rows

The page should feel designed section-by-section.

## Hero

Use a restrained, premium hero.

Large headline on the left or centered.

Keep the copy short.

Show a large real Olyxee product interface underneath or partially overlapping the hero.

Do not surround the hero with floating AI-generated cards or decorative gradients everywhere.

The product itself should be the visual.

## Freight Workflow Section

Instead of feature cards, create a wide visual workflow.

For example:

Customer
→ Quote
→ Order
→ Shipment
→ Tracking
→ Delivery

Use thin connecting lines, small operational labels, actual Olyxee UI components and subtle movement.

The workflow should feel like part of the software interface rather than an infographic.

## Product Sections

For each important part of the current product, use alternating split layouts.

Example:

### Quote to Order

Left:
large screenshot or cropped product interface.

Right:
small eyebrow label
strong headline
2–3 sentence explanation
one short value statement.

Then reverse the layout for the next section.

Example:

### Manage the Shipment

Copy left.

Large product UI right.

Do not put both capabilities into identical cards.

## Use Large Product Screens

At least 2–3 sections should feature a large product screenshot taking roughly 50–70% of the section width.

Show the actual system:

* quote screen
* order details
* air/sea shipment stages
* customer tracking page
* dashboard

Make screenshots feel integrated into the page with subtle borders, shadows and cropping.

Avoid generic laptop/device mockups unless they genuinely improve the composition.

## Strong Statement Sections

Between product sections, use short full-width statements.

Example:

### One job. One place to see what happened.

Supporting line:

Customer details, quotes, orders, payments and shipment progress stay connected.

No cards.

Just typography, whitespace and perhaps a small visual detail.

This creates visual breathing room.

## Pain Section

Make the pain section feel specific to freight forwarding.

Use an asymmetric layout.

Large copy on one side:

### Freight forwarders know how to move cargo.

The hard part is everything around it.

On the other side, show 3–4 compact operational pain rows:

* Shipment details scattered across messages and spreadsheets
* Customers repeatedly asking for updates
* Commercial and shipment information disconnected
* Too much time spent checking the same job

Do not turn these into four oversized generic icon cards.

Use smaller rows, dividers, numbers or interface-like elements.

## Customer Tracking Section

Make this visually different from the internal operations sections.

Use a light or contrasting background.

Show the customer-facing branded tracking experience prominently.

Copy:

### Give your customers visibility without giving them your internal workspace.

Show the tracking timeline, reference number and current shipment stage.

This section should make the product feel tangible.

## Free Plan Section

Do not use a huge three-column SaaS pricing table when there are only two plans.

Use an editorial pricing layout.

Left side:

### Start with the freight workflow you need today.

Short explanation.

Right side:

A clean Free plan panel:

R0/month

Current features.

Start Free button.

Then introduce Scale separately later in the page.

## Orgni Section

Visually change the mood here.

Use a darker or more immersive section.

Headline:

### Today you manage the workflow.

### Next, Orgni helps run it.

Show a future operations interface rather than a collection of AI feature cards.

Example interface:

68 active jobs
59 progressing normally
9 need attention

Then show 3–4 attention items:

Missing document
ETA changed
Payment blocking release
Clearance action required

The visual should communicate intelligence before the visitor reads the copy.

## Scale Section

Keep Scale connected visually to Orgni.

Do not make it another generic pricing card.

Use:

Scale
R1,499 / company / month

Then a short value statement:

**Handle more freight with less repetitive work.**

Show billing date clearly:

**Billing begins 30 September 2026.**

CTA:

Join Scale

## Social Proof

Use real people and businesses where available.

Prefer:

large testimonial quote
person/company information
small company logo

instead of a carousel of five tiny testimonials.

One believable story is stronger than many generic quotes.

## Section Transitions

Use subtle changes in:

* background tone
* spacing
* alignment
* screenshot placement
* typography scale

Do not add random gradients or decorative blobs simply to make sections look different.

The variation should come from composition.

## Visual Language

Keep it:

* minimal
* operational
* premium
* serious
* freight-specific
* product-first

Use:

* thin borders
* subtle shadows
* generous whitespace
* strong typography
* muted secondary text
* small operational labels/statuses
* restrained Olyxee accent color

Avoid:

* excessive glassmorphism
* generic glowing AI gradients
* floating icon cards
* unnecessary 3D graphics
* generic stock photos of trucks
* dozens of colorful feature icons
* every section being centered
* identical rounded cards everywhere

## Overall Feel

The page should feel like a designer deliberately composed every section around the story being told.

The visitor should experience different visual moments as they scroll:

**Big promise**
↓
**Real workflow**
↓
**Product close-up**
↓
**Operational pain**
↓
**Product solution**
↓
**Customer tracking**
↓
**Free plan**
↓
**Orgni future**
↓
**Scale**
↓
**Final CTA**

Take inspiration from mature freight software websites such as GoFreight and CargoWise in terms of section rhythm and product storytelling, but do not copy their layouts, branding or content.

Keep Olyxee visually distinct.
